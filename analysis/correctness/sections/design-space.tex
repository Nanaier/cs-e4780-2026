\subsection{Open questions in the specification}
\label{sec:design-space}

The brief fixes the formulas but leaves several behaviours open.
We implement the literal reading by default, put each alternative behind a switch that all engines share, and measure what it changes (Table~\ref{tab:design-space}).
Three choices change the output noticeably.

\paragraph{First-window advisories.}
With $\mathrm{EMA}_{j,w_0}=0$, a symbol's first closing price $c>0$ gives $\mathrm{EMA}_{38}=\frac{2c}{39} > \frac{2c}{101}=\mathrm{EMA}_{100}$ while both previous values are $0$, so the crossover rule fires a \emph{buy} for \emph{every} symbol in its first window, whatever the market does.
On the first real day (08.11.2021) all 4,840 advisories are such artefacts, one per symbol.
The weight of the initial zero in $\mathrm{EMA}_{100}$ decays as $(1-\frac{2}{101})^n$ and drops below 1\% only after $n=230$ windows, so the long average needs most of a trading day to reach the price level and genuine crossovers appear only from the second day.
We keep these advisories by default, because the specification defines them and the batch oracle must match it, but mark them: the switch \texttt{--suppress-first} drops the advisory of a symbol's first window, which removes 4,970 of the week's 11,349 advisories (one per symbol) and leaves 6,379 genuine crossovers, without changing any EMA value.
The dashboard hides them by default and shows them as grey ``warm-up'' markers on request (Figure~\ref{fig:dashboard}).
A subscriber that trades on the advisories should enable the switch; one that audits the specification should not.

\paragraph{Windows without trades.}
Many long-tail symbols have no trade in most 5-minute windows.
One reading, \texttt{skip}, emits nothing for such a window and carries the EMAs unchanged; the other, \texttt{repeat}, feeds the previous close again, as if the price had been observed.
\texttt{repeat} pulls both averages towards a stale price at different speeds and therefore creates crossovers that no trade caused: on three synthetic days it raises the advisories from 374 to 1,555 and makes the output volume proportional to the number of symbols times the number of windows, instead of to the trading activity.
We choose \texttt{skip}, as did the Grand Challenge solutions we compared with, because an EMA over windows that contain trades has a clear meaning and keeps the long tail cheap.
\texttt{repeat} remains available in the Python engine and the reference.

\paragraph{Time zone.}
The brief states times in CEST, but daylight saving time ended on 31.10.2021, so the trading week of 08--14.11.2021 is in CET (UTC+1).
We parse the trading date and time as naive local wall-clock time without conversion, as the official Grand Challenge loader does, and align windows to midnight local time; because 5 minutes divide a day, this equals alignment on the epoch.
The choice cannot change any EMA or advisory, only the labels of the windows, and the dashboard shows the exchange's wall-clock time so that windows line up with opening and closing auctions.
