\section{Conclusion}

We built a streaming system that detects EMA crossovers for every symbol of three exchanges over a full trading week, and showed that it is both correct and fast: three independent implementations agree exactly on 1.86 million EMA values and 11,349 advisories, and the Flink job processes about 1.6 million events per second on four cores with a median delay of about 0.2\,s after a window can close.
Treating correctness as a measurable property, through a batch oracle and a diff tool, made every optimisation and every new feature cheap to check.
The data set analysis paid off as much as the engineering: the out-of-orderness bound, the quarantine and the heartbeats all follow from measured properties of the data, not from guesses.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
All measurements ran on a single laptop with one Kafka broker, so the scaling results show how the job uses more cores, not how it behaves across machines and network links.
The Flink job implements only the \texttt{skip} policy for empty windows, and live subscription changes take effect from the next window close rather than immediately.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Natural next steps are a multi-node deployment with a replicated Kafka cluster, pushing results to the dashboard live over a WebSocket, and suppressing advisories until the EMAs are warmed up based on a confidence measure rather than a fixed rule.
